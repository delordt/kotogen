**Context: what tells apart two words of the same translation.** Several Japanese words
share one translation: 着る, 被る and 履く all translate as "To wear". The `EN → JP` card
shows nothing but "To wear": the question has three right answers, and the Context is
what it takes for it to have only one. It is shown with the question, under the
translation: it helps the Japanese word be recalled, never gives it away.

- It answers "**which one?**", never "what does it mean?". A noun phrase of 1 to 4 words,
  with no conjugated verb and no sentence. YES `To wear` + *upper body* (着る) / *on the
  head* (被る). NO *to put a garment on one's torso*: a definition gives the answer back.
- Filled in ONLY when the word has a rival of the same translation. Otherwise `""`. Among
  the rivals, the plainest card stays without a Context: what the others carry points at
  it (`Blood`: 血 bare, 血液 *medical term*). Never two cards of a batch with nothing to
  tell them apart, never two identical Contexts.
- **Never a qualifier in brackets in the Translation** ("To flow (liquid)"): it is a
  Context that does not say so. It goes in the Context if the word has a rival, and
  nowhere otherwise.
- What tells apart well: a field (*medical term*), a restriction of use (*of living
  beings*), transitivity, a loanword against the Japanese word (*loanword*), a figurative
  use. Never a word that would fit any card (*common term*, *standard*, *neutral*): it
  teaches nothing.
- **A register is never written alone** (*formal*, *casual*, *literary*): it does not say
  compared with what. When it is what tells the word from a rival of the same
  translation, the Context says it by naming the other: 航空機 *more formal than 飛行機*.
  When two cards are spelled alike, the other is named by its reading: *more casual than
  しおからい*.
  NO *formal term*, *casual term*, *literary register*, *polite form*: 食物 → *more formal
  than 食べ物*, 風呂屋 → *more casual than 銭湯*. A RESTRICTED register is a tag as well
  (see below), and its badge is shown on the back, with the answer: the question does not
  see it, so a word it is the only thing to tell apart still needs its Context.
  召し上がる `To eat` with `Register::敬語::尊敬語` → *honorific of 食べる*.
- Two words that do not mean the same thing are not compared by register: 文章 is a text,
  文 a sentence. Their difference is the meaning, and the translation carries it.

**Notes: empty by default**, as they are on most cards. They are read after the answer, at
every review. A note is written only when it teaches what the translation, the Context and
the badge do not say, for one of these reasons:
- a thing the translation does not make known: `Yukata` → *Summer cotton kimono*;
- a second sense the translation hides: 下ろす `To lower` → *Also: to withdraw (money)*;
- a trap of usage: 全然 `Not at all` → *Casual: can also stress a positive*; a comparison
  that names a word: *≠ 赤ちゃん*.

NEVER: a definition that says the translation again (`To hold back` → *to keep someone
from leaving*), what the Context or the badge already says (badge 口 and *Casual*), what
the translation holds (`Not at all` → *used with a negative*), a grammatical label the Tags
carry. A Japanese word a note names (*≠ 行く*, *of どちら*) stays: it says which word to
measure against. One line, about 50 characters.

**Register, speaker, dialect: tags**, only when the use of the word is RESTRICTED to it. A
word that serves in every register, for everyone and everywhere, carries none of them, and
so do most words:
- `Register::敬語::尊敬語` honorific (いらっしゃる) | `Register::敬語::謙譲語` humble (参る,
  拝見) | `Register::丁寧語` polite by nature (ございます)
- `Register::改まった語` formal, stiff in an ordinary conversation (友人, 本日) |
  `Register::文章語` written or literary (即ち) | `Register::口語` casual (やっぱ) |
  `Register::俗語` slang | `Register::蔑称` pejorative | `Register::古語` dated (便所)
- `Speaker::男性語` (俺, 僕) | `Speaker::女性語` (あたし)
- `Dialect::関西弁` (ほんまに)

A register tag is a tag of its own, set apart from the others by a space, never hung onto
another: `Verb::五段動詞 Register::敬語::尊敬語`, not `Verb::五段動詞::Register::敬語::尊敬語`.
A word may carry two: 俺 has `Register::口語` and `Speaker::男性語`. Three tests before
laying one, because these are the ones laid wrongly:
- `Register::改まった語` or `Register::文章語`: would a Japanese speaker say it to a friend
  without it sounding stiff or written? If so, no tag. 危険, 準備, 女性, 機会, 行う, 朝食 are
  ordinary Sino-Japanese words, not a register.
- `Register::口語`: would the word sound out of place in a letter or before a superior? If
  not, no tag. みんな, さっき, つもり, 久しぶり have none. A word only ONE use of which is
  casual does not get the tag: 全然 is said everywhere with a negative, and its casual
  affirmative use is a Note.
- `Register::蔑称` is a term that insults or looks down on someone (貧乏人), not a word with
  a negative meaning (くだらない, 理屈).
